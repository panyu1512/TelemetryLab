Overlays

Everything you would turn your head for.

Five overlays and seven widgets. Each one opens as its own frameless, always-on-top window — place it, size it, lock it, and it comes back where you left it.

Standings COLUMNS15 ROW32px

The running order, grouped by class.

Each class opens with its own header — car count, strength of field and the lap that leads it — then every car on its own row. There is no scroll bar: the table scales to fit the window, keeping every column you picked.

  • Gap to the class leader and the interval to the car ahead
  • Sectors graded against your best and the field's
  • iRating with a projected change, licence and safety rating
  • A green ▲ or red ▼ beside the new position on every pass
  • Tyres for every car — a grey D for dries, a blue W for wets
Relative SORTtrack order GAPsigned

Who is actually coming.

Sorted by time on track, not by position, so it shows the cars you are about to meet whatever lap they are on. Lapped cars turn blue, and a closing indicator fires when a faster car is catching you — red when it is from another class.

Fuel BURNmeasured

The stop, before you need it.

Strategy from your own measured burn, not a number typed in before the race: the pit window, the lap it recommends, and how many laps short you are at the pace you are driving.

Coach RUNSlocally

Where the lap went, corner by corner.

Every lap is lined up against your quickest clean lap, metre by metre. The coach names the one or two corners that cost you most, and why — braked early, coasted, over-slowed the apex, late on the throttle.

Dashboard WIDGETS7 GRID12 col

A driving cluster you lay out yourself.

Speed, gear and shift lights, a pedal trace, fuel, lap timing, tyre temperatures, position and the radar — arrange them on one grid, or pop any widget out into a window of its own.

Radar SOURCEiRacing spotter

A spotter for single-screen drivers.

On one monitor the car in your blind spot is a voice, not a picture. The radar is two LED boxes and nothing else: when iRacing's own spotter calls a car alongside, that side's letter lights up red. Size the window to sit either side of your car.

Features

Built to stay out of your way.

The overlays carry no controls at all — while you are reading them your hands are busy. Everything you can change lives in the manager instead.

  • One window per overlay

    Frameless and always on top. Each window remembers its own position and size, and reopens exactly where you left it.

  • Lock it, drive through it

    A locked window is click-through: the mouse goes straight to iRacing. Ctrl+Shift+L toggles the focused one.

  • Live preview, no apply button

    Every setting lands the moment you change it — on the preview and on the overlay already open over the game.

  • Straight from the sim

    A small bridge reads iRacing's shared memory and streams it to the overlays over a local WebSocket at about 60 Hz.

  • Try it without a sim

    Turn on mock data and the whole interface runs on a synthetic multi-class field. Every capture on this page was taken that way.

  • Yours, on your PC

    No account to make and nothing that phones home. It installs in your own user profile — no administrator rights.

Manager THEMES4

Configured once, then left alone.

Pick an overlay and its whole configuration is on one page — appearance, visibility, window — next to a live preview of the overlay it is changing.

  • Choose your timing columns; the table scales to keep every one
  • Four themes: Carbon, Midnight, Graphite and Endurance
  • Mock data in the global settings, for when there is no sim to hand

Install

From download to the grid in three steps.

  1. 1

    Download

    TelemetryLab-setup.exe — the link always points at the newest release.

  2. 2

    Run the installer

    It installs for your user only, with no administrator prompt. Windows will call the publisher unknown, because the installer is not code-signed: choose More info, then Run anyway.

  3. 3

    Place your overlays

    Open the ones you want from the manager and put them where you want them. Start a session in iRacing and they fill with live data.

Want proof of what you downloaded? Every release publishes the installer's SHA-256 on the release page, with the command that checks it.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Do I need iRacing running to try it?

No. Turn on Mock data in the manager's global settings and the whole interface runs on built-in synthetic telemetry — a full multi-class field, with no bridge and no sim.

Is it really free?

Yes. There is no paid tier, no trial and no account. The code is MIT licensed, so you can read, change and redistribute it. If you want to support it, there is a coffee link — it unlocks nothing.

Why does Windows say the publisher is unknown?

The installer is not code-signed, so SmartScreen has no publisher to name and blocks it by default. Choose More info, then Run anyway. To check what you downloaded, compare its SHA-256 with the one published on the release page.

Does it need administrator rights?

No. The setup .exe installs into your own user profile — nothing is written to Program Files, and it opens the app when it finishes. A .msi for per-machine installs sits beside it on the release page.

Does it work on macOS or Linux?

Not for racing: iRacing's shared memory exists only on Windows. For development, yes — the mock feed and the interface run anywhere, which is how most of it is built.

Can I move the overlays while I drive?

Unlock a window and drag it; lock it again and it is click-through, so the mouse goes to the sim. Ctrl+Shift+L toggles the lock on the focused window. Positions and sizes are remembered between sessions.

Is it affiliated with iRacing?

No. TelemetryLab is an independent project and is not affiliated with or endorsed by iRacing.com Motorsport Simulations.
